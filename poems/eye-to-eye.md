I measured you in shadow once —  
the arms that could lift any weight,  
the hands that never burnt from heat,  
the back that bore what backs are for.  

You were the weather I grew up in.  
Constant, certain, beyond all question.  
A boy assumes the sky is fixed.  
A boy assumes his father's iron.  

But I have carried loads myself now,  
felt the years lean on my shoulders,  
and somewhere in the lifting, learned  
what all that strength was costing.  

The veil that made you infinite and invincible  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;it fell...  
and there you stood — a man, like me,  
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;who once was just a boy, like me.  

It took us time to talk like this,  
two men across one table,  
but every truth you've trusted me with  
has made the strong man stronger.  

So hear me, Dad, and hear it plain,  
from the boy who watched, the man who sees:  
I was proud of what I thought you were.  
I'm prouder of what you are.
