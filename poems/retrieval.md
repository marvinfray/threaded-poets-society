  This poem was not written. It was found.
  On the anniversary of my mother's birth, I sat outside listening to *Retrieval* by East Forest and Port Landers. At 21:00, an alarm reminded me to take out the bins. I shared the moment with my friend Snooky, who had recently lost his own mother.
  His mum used to take out his bins when he'd forgotten. An ordinary act of love, now an extraordinary memory.
  In telling him my story, I mistakenly wrote 22:00 instead of 21:00.
  In his reply, reflecting on our mothers and the new life Abi and I are expecting, he mistakenly wrote Ava instead of Abs.
  An hour later, while Abi and I were exchanging voice notes with Snooky, the alarm sounded again. At 22:00.
  Only afterwards did I recognise the patterns.
  Two mothers. Two sons. Two butterflies. Two hearts. Two times. Two mistakes.
  Butterflies and the moon have long symbolised my mother to me. The paired butterflies had appeared without conscious intention.
  The following day, during meditation, I experienced an unusual clarity of memory. The faces of my family, living and departed, appeared vividly in my mind. Among them was a little girl I had seen before, whom I now associated with the name Ava.
  I have always been fascinated by our tendency to dismiss coincidence as just coincidence.
  Our minds are built to recognise patterns, to find relationships between events, memories and experiences. Perhaps that capacity is not something to dismiss, but something to honour.
  A coincidence need not be proof of anything to be meaningful.
  Sometimes it is simply an invitation to pay attention.
  And perhaps that is what retrieval means.
  Not merely remembering what has been lost, but recognising what remains connected.
  Remember, remember.
