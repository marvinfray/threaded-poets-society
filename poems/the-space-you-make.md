There is a heartbeat in our house  
that neither of us made alone.  
You carry it the way you carry  
everything — like it's already home.  

And I am watching you become  
what you have somehow always been:  
a mother, rising to the word  
before the word has settled in.  

I'll be honest, love: I'm frightened.  
"Father" is a mountain's name,  
and some days, climbing towards the word,  
my back almost falls beneath it.

But you make space for fear to speak  
without once letting it be king.  
You say "we've got this", like a fact.  
You say "you have this", like you've seen.  

And I am learning that my doubt  
is only love that hasn't found its door:  
the want to be my best for you,  
and for the one you're making room for.  

I don't know what I'd do without you,  
and I don't ever mean to learn.  
Beside you, all my crooked wanting  
has space to be seem the way I mean it to.

So take this poem as evidence —  
it only stands because you do.  
You are the strength in all my sentences,  
and my reason's carried forth in you.
